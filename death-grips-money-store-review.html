Death Grips - The Money Store: A Review

Death Grips exists in this sphere of elite rap and eccentric hip-hop - what you've probably heard called "music that scares the hoes." I decided to give this highly respected work a listen to see if it truly lives up to its legendary status. Spoiler: it mostly does.

Track-by-Track Breakdown:

Get Got - This is how you start an album. Fire beat, beautiful delivery, and striking visuals in the music video. Sets the tone perfectly. 10/10

The Fever - Good energy with skilled beat and production. It hurts that I'm struggling to understand MC Ride sometimes, but I know it's heat. The music video shows their creative vision once again. 8/10

Lost Boys - Too much going on, and not in a good way. The production stays consistent though, which is something Death Grips never fails at. 5/10

Blackjack - My god, they came for blood on this one. The tone, energy, and instrumental create something special. With no truly poor lines from MC Ride, the song elevates itself through already great production into a higher echelon. The drums and bass were absolutely crazy. 10/10

Hustle Bones - Unique visuals and a masterclass from MC Ride. The music video encapsulates the song perfectly with his movement. WHAT WHAT. 10/10

I've Seen Footage - Beautiful work with that riff. The video, while fitting the band's aesthetic, feels like it's missing something. I love the effort though. I'VE SEEN. 10/10

Double Helix - These videos showcase some of the best creative ideas I've seen. This track calms things down a bit from the intensity. 6/10

System Blower - He's picking the energy back up here. The production hasn't failed, and neither has MC Ride's delivery. 7/10

Punk Weight - It's good, but lyrically just not my thing. 7/10

Fuck That - Gives me Hotline Miami vibes. Feels like a last-second addition to the tracklist. 4/10

Bitch Please - Sometimes MC Ride has these moments where he achieves the pinnacle of his voice, and it definitely happens here. His anger and directness can be felt throughout. With other musicians, that intensity can feel performative or lack consistency, but for MC Ride, it's his standard. Only thing stopping this from being a 10 is the chorus - those verses were clean though. 8/10

Hacker - This is crazy work for 2012. I'm in your AREA. 8/10

Final Thoughts:

Death Grips is probably the most influential sound to come out of the underground, other than Playboi Carti. The era of Yeezus and The Money Store should've been appreciated more when it dropped. This album lives up to the hype.

Overall Score: 7.75/10
